Cause & Effect Analysis for High Average Handle Time

Problem Statement

Average Handle Time (AHT) in the support center has increased to about 9 minutes per call. This analysis aims to list and organize potential causes before we test them with data. Fishbone (Ishikawa) diagrams and Why‑Why questions are common root‑cause tools in the Analyze phase. [web:595][web:608]

Fishbone Categories and Potential Causes

People

  • New agents are not fully confident on complex topics.
  • Senior agents get many escalations and are overloaded.
  • Different agents use different call flows.

Process

  • Call flow is not documented in one simple place.
  • Many calls are transferred between “general” and “billing” queues.
  • Wrap‑up notes are long and sometimes duplicated in two systems.

Technology

  • Knowledge base search is slow and results are not well ranked.
  • Agents switch between three systems to see full customer history.
  • Occasional “system not responding” errors cause re‑entry of data.

Policy / Measurement

  • Agents are told “never leave a question unanswered” but have no time limits.
  • Quality reviews focus on call friendliness more than call efficiency.
  • No clear rule on when to transfer versus when to own a case.

Why‑Why Mini‑Chains

Example 1: Long “research” time

  1. Why are research steps long? → Agents take time to find the right article.
  2. Why is it hard to find the right article? → Search results show many outdated entries.
  3. Why are outdated entries still shown? → There is no regular review or archive process.

Example 2: Many transfers

  1. Why are there many transfers? → Agents are unsure if they are allowed to resolve billing issues.
  2. Why are they unsure? → Policy is vague and training examples are limited.
  3. Why is policy vague? → The billing process changed, but training material was not updated.

Data Collected to Check Causes

To avoid guessing, the team collected a small but focused data set of 50 calls:

  • Call type (general question, billing, refund, technical).
  • AHT for each call.
  • Whether the call was transferred (yes/no).
  • Number of times the agent opened the knowledge base.

How the Data Was Interpreted

Simple averages showed that:

  • Calls with at least one transfer were on average 4 minutes longer.
  • Billing and refund calls had the highest average AHT.
  • Calls where the agent opened the knowledge base more than 3 times were almost always in the “long call” group.

These patterns support the earlier Why‑Why ideas: unclear ownership of billing questions and slow or hard‑to‑use knowledge search are strong candidates for real root causes.
